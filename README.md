# Before AI handles people's money and benefits: what has to be settled first

*October 2026. Not financial or legal advice.*

AI assistants are starting to touch real money. They have their own wallets, links to bank accounts, and trading accounts. The next step people want is obvious: "Here's a little money, help me make more, and don't mess up my benefits." Several of the biggest AI companies are moving toward some version of this.

This page is not a plan. It's a list of things that have to be considered, and answered with confidence, before a system like that is put in place. The main argument is simple:

**A system that handles people's money and benefits cannot be allowed to make things up.**

Today's AI models, Claude included, can still state wrong things with confidence. In a chat, that's an annoyance. If an AI gets a benefit rule, a tax bracket or a savings limit wrong, it can cost someone their food assistance, their health coverage or their rent. The tolerance for made-up information here has to be zero.

## Why this needs coordination, not one company

The rules people live under are spread across many systems:

- federal agencies
- 50 state systems, which often disagree with each other
- income brackets and wage rules
- benefit programs, each with its own limits

If each AI company builds its own version, people get several different answers to the same question, and nobody is accountable for which one is right.

These need to be settled together, by:

- **the major AI companies**, such as Anthropic, OpenAI and Google
- **lawmakers**, including the U.S. Senate
- **the federal and state agencies** that run these programs

The goal is one shared approach that works inside the systems that already exist, rather than around them.

## The areas that have to be covered

| Area | What has to be assured first | Who has to be involved |
|---|---|---|
| **No made-up information** | Every rule, limit and number comes from an official source, is checked against the current version, and is shown with that source. When the system isn't sure, it says so and stops. | AI companies, the agencies that publish the rules |
| **Benefit limits** | The system knows each program's limits for that person's state and situation before any money moves. | SSA (SSI), USDA (SNAP), CMS and state Medicaid offices, HUD (housing) |
| **Income, taxes and wages** | Earnings are counted the way each program and tax bracket counts them, including tax credits people might lose. | IRS, state tax agencies, Department of Labor |
| **Investing rules** | No promised returns. A shared pool of people's money is a regulated security, and only licensed firms can manage it. | SEC, FINRA, state securities regulators |
| **Holding and moving money** | Whoever holds the money has the licenses to do it, and people's money is protected if that company fails. | FinCEN, state money regulators, CFPB |
| **Permission** | The person sets clear limits, the AI can't change them, and every money action gets a human yes. | AI companies, banks, card networks |
| **The information gap** | The system asks about things the person may not think to mention: benefits, debts, court orders, other income. It never assumes. | AI companies, benefits advocates |
| **A record of everything** | Every action is logged by the system itself, not described by the AI, and the person can see it and stop it. | AI companies |
| **Privacy** | Benefit and income details stay private, optional and deletable, and are never used to train AI. | AI companies, FTC, state privacy laws |
| **Scams** | People can always tell the real system from a copy. Nothing ever asks for passwords in a chat. | FTC, card networks, AI companies |
| **Fairness and access** | Safety rules don't lock out the people who need help most, and people with better AI don't get unfair advantages for limited help. | Lawmakers, agencies, advocates |

## Facts that show why each area matters

**Benefit limits are strict and easy to cross**
- SSI stops if a person's countable savings go over **$2,000** ($3,000 for a couple), a limit unchanged since 1989. ([Justice in Aging](https://justiceinaging.org/the-ssi-resource-limit-and-common-solutions/))
- ABLE accounts, for people who qualify, don't count toward that limit up to $100,000. ([ABLE National Resource Center](https://www.ablenrc.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/FactSheetOnSSIResourceLimitsAndCommonSolutions.pdf))
- SNAP's federal limit is $3,000, or $4,500 with an older or disabled household member, but 46 states change it through their own rules. ([USDA](https://fns-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/resource-files/BBCE-States-Chart-June2026.pdf))
- An AI that "grows" someone's money without knowing these rules can do more harm than good.

**Promised returns are the top fraud signal**
- The SEC lists promised or "guaranteed" returns as the first red flag. ([Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-alerts/investor-54))
- Pooling people's money with an expectation of profit from someone else's work makes it a security under U.S. law. ([Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/HoweyCoins))

**Safe money grows slowly**
- In mid-2026, savings accounts and short Treasury bills paid roughly 3.6% to 4.1% a year. ([InvestorMint](https://investormint.com/uncategorized/treasury-bills-vs-high-yield-savings-2026))
- At that rate, $10 earns about 40 cents in a year. Anything promising much more is taking risk.

**The pieces already exist, separately**
- **AI wallets** with limits the AI can't change (Coinbase, February 2026), plus agent cards from Visa, Mastercard, Stripe and others. ([eco.com](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/14845485-coinbase-agentic-wallets-explained), [Privacy.com](https://www.privacy.com/blog/payment-solutions-ai-agents-2026-compared))
- **AI connected to trading accounts.** Robinhood reportedly allows this in separate accounts, though reports conflict. ([Finder](https://www.finder.com/stock-trading/best-agentic-trading-platforms))
- **AI reading bank accounts.** Anthropic is reportedly testing a feature that links bank accounts to Claude for read-only analysis. ([TestingCatalog](https://www.testingcatalog.com/anthropic-prepares-claude-money-for-personal-finance/))
- **AI helping benefit caseworkers.** Code for America and Anthropic built a Claude tool to help SNAP staff interpret policy. ([Government Executive](https://www.govexec.com/technology/2026/05/anthropic-code-america-pilot-ai-tools-snap/413464/))
- None of these were designed together. That's the gap.

**Sharing AI's gains is already being discussed**
- **Anthropic** committed 5% of its shares to a Public Benefit Fund in October 2026, with more as the company's value grows. ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/public-benefit-fund))
- **OpenAI** proposed giving 5% of its equity to a U.S. sovereign wealth fund. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/02/openai-proposed-donating-5-of-its-equity-to-a-us-sovereign-wealth-fund/))
- **A Senate bill from June 2026** would fund yearly payments to Americans from AI company shares. ([Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/06/18/sovereign-wealth-fund-tax-on-ai-companies-unveiled-by-sanders/))
- **Anthropic's Economic Futures program** funds research and policy trials on sharing AI's benefits broadly. ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/economic-futures/program))
- These efforts are the natural place to work out how "everyone gets something back" could happen legally.

## Open questions

- Who is responsible when an AI gets a benefit rule wrong and someone loses coverage?
- How do agencies publish their rules so AI systems can check them directly, instead of relying on what a model remembers?
- How does a person prove they qualify for something without handing over their whole financial life?
- How do safety limits protect people without shutting out the people who need help most?
- Which body keeps all of this consistent across companies and states?
